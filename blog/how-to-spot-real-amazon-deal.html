How to Spot a Real Amazon Deal (vs. a Fake Discount)

September 28, 2026

Not every "70% OFF" is a deal. Some sellers inflate the list price so the discount looks dramatic. Here's the five-minute checklist I use before posting anything to Today's Deals:

1. Check the price history

Paste the product URL into a price tracker (CamelCamelCamel is the classic free one). If the "sale" price is what it sold for all month, it's not a deal — it's marketing. Real deals dip clearly below the 90-day average.

2. Interrogate the list price

Amazon's crossed-out "list price" is often the manufacturer's suggested price, not what anyone actually paid. A $44.99 list price on an item that's been $14.99 for months is fiction. Compare against the typical selling price, not the strikethrough.

3. Read the 3-star reviews

Five-star reviews can be gamed; one-star reviews are often user error. Three-star reviews are where the honest trade-offs live — "works great but runs small," "good for the price but loud." Ten minutes here saves more money than any coupon.

4. Check the seller

On the product page, look at "Sold by." Amazon itself or the brand's official store is safest. Third-party sellers with thin feedback histories are where counterfeits and bait-and-switch listings hide — especially on big-ticket electronics.

5. Stack everything

Real deal hunters layer: sale price + clipped coupon + promo code at checkout + credit-card or Prime Rewards points. The posted price is the starting bid, not the final answer.

The shortcut

Doing this for every purchase is exhausting — which is why Today's Deals exists. Every product there passed this checklist before it got posted, and prices get re-verified every morning.
