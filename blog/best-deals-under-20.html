Buying tips

The trick to cheap finds is remembering that the real cost of a bad $12 purchase isn't $12 — it's $12 plus the $30 replacement you buy next month. Favor simple products with few failure modes (a paste, a cable, a hand tool) over cheap electronics with batteries and apps. Check the rating distribution, not just the average: a 4.4-star product with thousands of reviews is a safer bet than a 4.9 with forty. And when two picks look identical, buy the one from the brand you've heard of — in this tier, brand accountability is worth more than a dollar or two of savings.

If you only buy three things on this page

Start with the cleaning paste. It's the pick with the widest usefulness and the longest life, and it's the one most likely to convert a skeptic into a regular reader of this page — a tub that rescues a pan you were about to throw away does that. Second, the rechargeable air duster if you work at a desk or own anything with a fan in it; it retires a recurring purchase, and retiring a recurring purchase is the purest form of saving money.

Third, whichever of the remaining picks matches a date on your calendar: the advent calendar before December, the necklaces before the next birthday you're likely to forget, the steamers before you need a small, decent gift in a hurry. That's the real pattern behind this whole list — buy the useful thing slightly before you need it, at the price it's actually discounted to, and the under-$20 tier stops being a junk drawer and starts being a system.
