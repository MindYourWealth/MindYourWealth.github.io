About Mind Your Wealth

Mind Your Wealth is a small, independently run deals site. The idea is simple: most "deal" content on the internet is scraped, stale, or written around whatever pays the highest commission. This site tries to do the opposite — a short list of Amazon deals and gift guides, picked by a person, checked by hand, and refreshed every day.

What you'll find here

The Today's Deals page is the daily board: a rotating set of Amazon products whose prices were verified live that morning. When a price moves back up or a coupon expires, the deal comes down — a smaller list of real deals beats a long list of dead ones.

The blog is where the slower, more useful work lives: budget-first buying guides like the best kitchen gadgets under $25, gifts under $25 for men and for women, stocking stuffers under $15, and self-care gifts under $30 — plus explainers like how to spot a real Amazon deal instead of a fake discount.

How a deal earns its spot

  1. Recognizable brands first. A discount only saves you money if the product is worth owning. We favor established brands with deep review histories over no-name listings with inflated "was" prices.
  2. The price is checked live. Before anything is posted, its price is read on Amazon that day — not copied from a feed, a screenshot, or yesterday's notes. If the discount can't be confirmed, the deal doesn't run with a price at all.
  3. The discount has to be real. We compare against the price the item actually sells for, not just the strikethrough list price. A "70% off" claim against a list price nobody has paid in months is marketing, not a deal.
  4. Guides are built by budget. Most people shop with a number in mind — a $15 stocking, a $25 gift, a $30 treat. Our guides are organized that way, with honest pros and cons, a comparison table, and answers to the questions buyers actually ask.

How we make money

Mind Your Wealth is reader-supported through the Amazon Associates program. When you buy through a link on this site, Amazon pays us a small commission on the purchase — at no extra cost to you. That disclosure appears on every page, because it should. Commissions never decide what gets picked: a deal earns its spot by being worth your money first, and plenty of excellent products never appear here simply because they weren't genuinely discounted when we checked.
